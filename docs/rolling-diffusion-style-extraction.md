# Rolling Diffusion style extraction

This theme is an original system inferred from the two user-provided academic
figures. It captures recurring design principles without reproducing their exact
layout, labels, or scientific content.

## Observed language

- Dense white technical canvas divided into soft-gray rounded regions.
- Dark charcoal header rails with clipped/slanted ends and white panel labels.
- Neutral charcoal display hierarchy rather than colored headings.
- Predominantly gray computation modules and robot stages, with color confined to
  state/action sequences, paths, compact tags, and focal borders.
- Low-saturation navy blue for state histories and structural decoration; orange
  and yellow-orange for reconstruction, current/future emphasis, and focal paths;
  restrained burgundy for action history and denoising residuals. Purple is not
  part of this extracted palette.
- Rounded data rows and narrow vertical semantic rails; tapered blocks for learned
  encoders/decoders; cylinders for datasets; black directed connectors; gray dotted
  alignment/provenance guides.
- Explicit past/future timelines, phase bands, token sequences, and small repeated
  marks that show diffusion or rollout progression.

## Adaptation decisions

The new theme converts appearance into semantic tokens, reduces gradients to
print-safe flat fills, keeps shadows optional, and targets a 70–80% neutral visual
field. Orange is the principal accent, while low-saturation blue supports structure
without dominating it. Burgundy is limited to action/diffusion history. It adds
cross-media rules: clipped headers become
web section rails and video chapter cards; timelines become scrub/progress motifs;
signal colors activate in motion while neutral machinery stays spatially stable.

The source figures are treated as visual references supplied by the user. Their
exact content, composition, branding, and robot imagery are not included.
